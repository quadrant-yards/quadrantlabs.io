Three directions from the Chesapeake

Each palette starts from something real about the brand's origins: the Hartge yard, the Bay itself, and the quadrant as a navigation instrument. The mark, wordmark (Jost) and body type (Inter) stay the same, so only color changes between them. Each one is shown on the marketing site and in a product UI, in light and dark.

ABoatyard

Hartge Yacht Yard, 1956. Varnished mahogany, sailcloth, copper bottom paint, bronze fittings and pine tar. A wooden boat made by hand.

Feels: warm, handmade, heritage · Risk: can read "rustic" or old-fashioned for a tech consultancy

CChart Room

A nautical chart of the Bay: buff paper, pale-blue shallows, deep-navy soundings, and the magenta that NOAA charts use for aids to navigation. A quadrant is a navigation tool and a fractional CTO helps a team find its bearings, so the chart metaphor fits the work.

Feels: precise, distinctive, quietly clever · Risk: magenta needs discipline: used sparingly it's a signature, used too much it's loud

What to look for

Choose the one you'd still like after seeing it every day for five years, not the one that's most striking here. Pay attention to the product card, because the apps will show the palette far more often than the marketing site will.

Mixing is fine, e.g. "C's neutrals with A's accent." Once a direction is chosen, I'll lock the exact values, check every text/background pair for contrast (WCAG AA), and add product accent colors.
