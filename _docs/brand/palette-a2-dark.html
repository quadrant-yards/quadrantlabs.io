Fixing Livery's dark mode

In the original dark mode the boat changed. The deep bottom-paint red turned into a light coral, the navy waterline stripe faded to pale denim, and the background went cool blue while light mode is neutral.

All three fixes keep the red deep for solid areas (buttons, bars, stripe) with white text on it, and use a lighter red only for small text. They differ in the background color, which is the "hull color" of the boat.

Light (reference)

White topsides, navy boot stripe, deep red bottom paint.

Current dark

Coral buttons with dark text, pale stripe, cool background. This is the version that felt off.

D1 · Split red

Smallest change: same background, but the red is fixed (deep fills, light red text only). Shows how much of the problem was the red alone.

D2 · Navy hull

Many boats have dark navy hulls. Here the background becomes the navy and the waterline stripe flips to white. Light and dark mode read as two boats from the same yard.

D3 · Black hull

A neutral black with a slight warmth, matching the neutral white of light mode. Off-white stripe. Closest to a direct inversion of light mode.

The rule this adds to the guide

Brand red has two roles. Fill red (#A3261C light, #B3291E dark) covers solid areas and always has white text on it. Text red is a lighter version used only for red words on a dark background. Never use the lighter red as a fill.

Every text pair here meets WCAG AA (4.5:1 or better). White on the dark-mode fill red is 6.5:1.
